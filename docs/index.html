Features

Convenient history, kept private.

Everything stays on your Mac, with no server, no account and no network code.

Secret detection built in

Skips items flagged concealed by password managers, Luhn-valid card numbers, known key prefixes like ghp_ and AKIA, and long high-entropy tokens.

Items expire automatically

Choose a lifetime of 1 minute, 5 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day or never. Expired items are removed from disk on the next write.

Encrypted at rest

History is sealed with AES-GCM. The 256-bit key lives in your login keychain, not next to the data.

Optional Touch ID lock

Require Touch ID or your account password to view history. It re-locks after 60 seconds of inactivity and when the screen locks.

Searchable, click to copy

The menu shows your 10 most recent clips. Show All opens a searchable list, and clicking an entry copies it back.

Control over what is captured

Ignore specific apps, pause capture, clear everything, and set history size from 10 to 500 entries. Launch at login is optional.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

PrivateClipVault is open source and not notarized with a paid Apple Developer ID, so macOS needs one extra step the first time.

  1. Download PrivateClipVault-macOS.zip and unzip it.
  2. Move PrivateClipVault.app to your Applications folder.
  3. Clear the quarantine flag once, then right-click the app and choose Open:
    xattr -cr /Applications/PrivateClipVault.app
  4. No special permissions are required macOS may ask you to approve Login Items in System Settings General only if you enable Launch at login.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is PrivateClipVault really free and open source?

Yes. It is released under the MIT license, and the full source is on GitHub. There are no ads, accounts, analytics or paid tiers.

What permissions does it need?

None of the privacy permissions: no Accessibility, Screen Recording or Microphone access. It uses the keychain for its own encryption key, and Touch ID only if you turn on the lock.

Where is my data stored?

In an encrypted file at ~/Library/Application Support/PrivateClipVault/vault.sealed on your Mac. The key is in your login keychain, and nothing is ever sent over the network.

Why isn't the app notarized?

Notarization requires a paid Apple Developer account, which this free project does not have. Right-click the app and choose Open once to approve it, or build it yourself from source.

Was this written by AI?

Yes. PrivateClipVault was generated by Claude Code from a written spec as a clean-room reimplementation, with no code or assets from any commercial app. The full source is on GitHub under MIT, so you can read every line before you run it.
